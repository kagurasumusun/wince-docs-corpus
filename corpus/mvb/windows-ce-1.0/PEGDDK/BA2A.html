About Pegasus Device Drivers

Pegasus device drivers are defined as either built-in drivers or installable drivers. Built-in drivers support the hardware components that are integrated into the computer. Installable drivers adapt the basic configuration to support other devices accessed through the built-in PC Card slot or serial port. Installable drivers typically rely on the built-in drivers for underlying support.

Examples of built-in drivers include the keyboard driver, display driver, touch-screen driver, audio driver, infrared driver, serial driver, and PC Card driver. These drivers are considered built-in because, unlike a PC, a Pegasus device does not have an expansion bus. The design of each built-in device is different for each original equipment manufacturer (OEM) and the built-in drivers are developed by the OEM. Information on creating a built-in driver is beyond the scope of this DDK.

Examples of installable drivers include those for an SRAM or Pager PC Card. This DDK focuses on creating drivers for the installable devices that extend system functionality through the PC Card slot or serial port.

PC Card Drivers

Installable PC Card drivers are actually clients of the built-in PC Card driver. Each computer will have a built-in PC Card driver designed to work with the physical PC Card hardware adapter. While the design of each PC Card driver varies from device to device, they all expose a common, well-defined interface—the Card Services interface. Both installable drivers and applications that require the PC Card driver services use this interface. Figure 1 illustrates the relationships among a client PC Card driver or application, Card Services, Socket Services, and the hardware adapter. Unlike a PC, the Socket Services layer is not exposed. For more information on the Card Services interface, see Chapter 2, “The Device Manager” and Chapter 4, “Card Services Reference.”

Serial Drivers

Installable serial drivers are client drivers. The serial hardware for each computer will have a built-in serial driver designed specifically to support the unique hardware. Installable serial drivers access the built-in serial driver to use the serial interface. Thus, installable serial drivers expose the Win32 communications interface for their operation.

Using the Touch Screen

Instead of a mouse, the HPC is equipped with a touch screen. Users are expected to interact with the screen using a stylus provided by the manufacturer. In practice, a ballpoint pen works quite well and may actually be used by many. A finger can also be used, but it is much less precise.

The touch screen provides much the same functionality as a mouse, and can be handled in a similar fashion in your program. Touching the screen with the stylus generates a WM_LBUTTONDOWN message and releasing it generates WM_LBUTTONUP. Tapping twice in rapid succession generates WM_LBUTTONDBLCLK. The right button equivalents are generated by tapping while holding the ALT key down (which generates WM_RBUTTONDOWN and so on). There is no equivalent to the middle button.

Porting left button operations will thus require virtually no alteration. However, given the awkwardness of generating right button commands, you may wish to consider alternatives. Most of the code samples included in the SDK give examples of how to manage the touch screen (Generic and EtchaSketch, are good starting points).

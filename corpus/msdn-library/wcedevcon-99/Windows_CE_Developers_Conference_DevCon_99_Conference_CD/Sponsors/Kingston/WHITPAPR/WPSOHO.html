Small Office Home Office Computing

Kingston's SOHO (Small Office Home Office) hubs are designed to provide user-friendly network connectivity that meets the needs of Small Office Home Office users. By providing cost effective, easy-to-use Ethernet connectivity Kingston is making it possible for small business users and home users alike to take advantage of the increased productivity that comes from the efficient use of information resources.

The desire to share expensive computing resources has been the driving force behind the growth of networks both in large and small computing environments. Organizations realize they can save time and money by connecting their systems together to share data and resources, including printers and modems.

Historically, thin coaxial cable has been the preferred method of cabling for small Workgroup networks. Solutions based on coaxial cabling were less expensive relative to Unshielded Twisted Pair networks because they did not require the use of a hub to connect the PCs together. Since early hubs were designed to meet the needs of large corporate networks, unnecessary features made them cost prohibitive for use in small network environments.

Today, the vast majority of new network installations are for Small Office/Home Office environments and involve fewer than 8 PCs. With that in mind, Kingston designed the EtheRx SOHO hub to meet the unique requirements of smaller network installations.

Features and Functions

Compact Desktop Design

To accommodate the wide range of Small Office Home Office operating environments the EtheRx SOHO units are designed for the widest possible array of installation options. They are compact enough to place on desktops and they ship with VelcroTM for wall-mounting or convenient fastening to network servers.

Simplified Network Troubleshooting

Troubleshooting coax networks are difficult because the entire coaxial cable segment must be checked by going from node to node checking the entire cable distance (up to 185 meters). In contrast, troubleshooting an EtheRx SOHO 10BASE-T network for cable problems involves only checking the cable connection from the EtheRx

SOHO hub to the node experiencing problems. In addition, because the EtheRx SOHO hub is fully compliant with IEEE 802.3 repeater functions, it features jabber lock, auto-partition and auto-reconnection. Should a system experience problems the "jabber" will not tie up the entire network. Instead, the EtheRx SOHO will recognize this "jabber" as noise, then lock and automatically partition the stations connection from the network until it has been repaired. Should the problem be temporary the auto-reconnection feature will automatically re-establish the stations connection to the network.

Versatile Power Supply Options

Fig. 3 Selecting the Power Connector

Because flexibility is the ultimate goal, the EtheRx SOHO hubs can be powered by either a 120VDC (U.S.) or 230VDC (European) power supply. Kingston also offers an optional in-line Keyboard Port Power Cable (available upon request) which uses power from the keyboard port of a computer. Simply slide the cover from left to right to access either power connection.

Built-in Network Expansion Capability

Fig. 4 Cable Switch location

A built-in crossover switch provides wiring flexibility on the first UTP Port 1 for connecting to a workstation or cascading to another Ethernet hub. Should your network grow, simply switch the crossover port into cascade connection on UTP Port 1 and connect another EtheRx SOHO hub. You may establish up to 28 network connections by cascading up to 4 EtheRx SOHO hubs.

 

Glossary

10BASE-T - An implementation of the IEEE 802.3 Ethernet standard on 26 to 22-gauge unshielded twisted-pair wiring, a Baseband medium, at 10 megabits per second.

802.3 - Defined by the IEEE, these standards govern the use of the CSMA/CD network access method used by Ethernet networks.

Automatic Partitioning/Reconnection - If any of the ports on the EtheRx SOHO experience excessive collisions or faulty conditions, that port will be partitioned. If the error conditions are corrected, the hub automatically reconnects that port to the network.

Crossover Cable - A cable connecting or cascading between two hubs. The transmit and receive pins on these cables are crossed.

Hub - An Ethernet repeater that brings together connections from multiple network nodes.

Jabber Lock - A protection scheme to ensure that the network is not disabled due to transmission of excessively long or damaged data packets.

Link Test - A link test pulses which checks for a valid connection with all RJ-45 twisted-pair ports.

RG-58 - A type of shielded coaxial cable used for "thin" Ethernet transmission (thinnet).

RJ-45 - A modular eight-wire connector used to terminate unshielded twisted-pair cable.

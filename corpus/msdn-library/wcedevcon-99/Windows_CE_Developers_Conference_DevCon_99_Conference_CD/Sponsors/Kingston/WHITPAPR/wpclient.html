Features And Benefits Of PCI Systems

Less than 3 years ago most PCs featured an Industry Standard Architecture (ISA) bus which has a 16-bit bus width and a maximum throughput of up to Eight Megabytes Per Second (8Mbps). This was more than sufficient for DOS and other less graphics intensive applications. With today’s 32-bit bandwidth intensive applications, however, traditional ISA bus adapters can create bottlenecks and limit productivity.

The introduction of the Pentium processor in 1994 marked the end of the venerable ISA bus, which was not equipped to support developing 64-bit processor architectures. As a result, the Intel designed and publicly released Peripheral Component Interconnect (PCI) reference specification quickly became the standard used by motherboard manufacturers developing new systems

The PCI bus features a full 32-bit bus width and provides up to 132Mbps maximum throughput. PCI Local Bus Architecture allows peripheral devices to directly access CPU resources with minimal latency. Designed to make system configuration easy, PCI’s inherent Plug-and-Play functionality dynamically assigns system resources including I/O port addresses, IRQs, and DMA channels making installation easy and eliminating conflicts with other system peripherals.

The PCI bus offers a better performance balance between the CPU and the expansion bus than earlier bus architectures. Physically, the PCI expansion bus is treated as a chip on the motherboard with no division between the local and peripheral buses, resulting in better I/O performance. PCI also provides for concurrency, meaning the bus can execute more than one function simultaneously, such as data transfer over Ethernet and a floating point calculation resulting in better performance than bus architectures that do not support concurrency.

Why PCI Ethernet Adapters

Today’s PCI systems come equipped with two buses: the high speed PCI Local bus consisting of usually three to four slots and a slower expansion bus that is backward compatible with ISA, EISA or Micro Channel adapters. Historically, the PCI bus slots have been populated by system integrators, resellers and end users with peripherals requiring high data transfer rates, such as graphics adapters and SCSI hard drive controllers.

The data transfer capabilities of the PCI bus are also ideal for eliminating the bottlenecks caused by today's 32-bit, bandwidth intensive applications and large data files, including imaging and CAD files. The PCI bus's high clock speed and 32-bit data path allow for high-speed data transfers without the time and expense of network segmentation, switching technology, or revamping the whole network with 100Mbps technology. Unlike 16-bit ISA Ethernet adapters, PCI Ethernet adapters feature a full 32-bit software structure (i.e. 32-bit registers, internal 32-bit data transfers, 32-bit data transfer to the bus, and 32-bit addressing capability). Therefore PCI Ethernet adapters optimize the data transfer capabilities of the PCI bus and eliminate the bottlenecks large data files cause in machines using ISA based Ethernet adapters.

Despite the performance advantages of PCI Ethernet adapters, networking professionals have traditionally opted to install ISA adapters in the expansion bus of PCI systems because of their lower cost relative to PCI adapters. As PCI Ethernet adapter prices continue to decline and ISA slots become increasingly limited, networking professionals are taking advantage of the significant performance increases offered by PCI adapters.

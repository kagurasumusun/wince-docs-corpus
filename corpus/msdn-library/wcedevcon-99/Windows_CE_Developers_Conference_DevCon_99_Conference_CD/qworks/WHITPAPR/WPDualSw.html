Overview
KNS400/R, KNS800/R

Kingston’s EtheRx 10/100Mbps Fast Ethernet switches are high-performance, rack-mountable switching hubs ideal for the small-to-medium business. Model KNS800/R supports up to 4K MAC addresses per port, for a total maximum of 32K MAC addresses, while the KNS400/R supports up to 8K MAC addresses. Both provide automatic switching between 10Mbps and 100Mbps operation. The KNS400/R has four (4) and the KNS800/R has eight (8) 10/100BASE-TX UTP ports that auto-negotiate 10/100Mbps operation, thus allowing standard Ethernet networks to communicate with Fast Ethernet networks, and to segment both 10Mbps and 100Mbps networks. They support collision-based flow control and use the Store-and-Forward switching approach to minimize on the re-transmission of faulty packets and provide dedicated service and guaranteed bandwidth for both 10Mbps and 100Mbps segments. Both comply with IEEE802.3u 100BASE-TX, IEEE802.3i 10BASE-T, and IEEE802.3 CSMA/CD standards.

Overview

As companies grow, so does the demand for bandwidth and increased performance to accommodate additional workload. Although companies would like to improve the performance of their network it is often a costly venture which requires replacing wiring, hardware and administration. An alternative solution is changing to a complete new networking technology such as Fiber or ATM, which are also costly and time-consuming solutions. More companies today are opting to turn to switch technology, which allows them to utilize their existing network infrastructure while adding the bandwidth and speed necessary to relieve network bottlenecks caused by increased user demand.

The Problem

To understand how a switch operates it is first important to look at the dynamics of a typical network. A network enables two or more users to communicate with one another and share resources. Messages or packets of information are passed back and forth via a pathway, which is much like a single lane highway. The speed limit of the highway in which information is allowed to pass is referred to as bandwidth and is measured in megabits. For example, a 10-megabit per second (Mbps) network has only 1/10th the amount of bandwidth as a 100 megabit per second network. Much like our rush hour traffic jams; networks also become congested when too many users are trying to communicate with one another at the same time within the 10Mbps bandwidth.

To alleviate network bottlenecks, companies are migrating from 10 to 100 Mbps to increase both the speed at which packets are sent and bandwidth. This too, can become a costly undertaking and is more easily accomplished with a dual speed product, which allows a 10 Mbps network to be connected to 100 Mbps. This permits the company to gradually migrate their network as their time and budget permits.

The Solution

Switch technology has become the easiest and most cost effective solution to an overworked network. It operates in two ways to relieve network bottlenecks. First, it segments the network to provide dedicated bandwidth to each user. Whereas on a typical network all the users share the available bandwidth, a switch provides each user, or groups of users, with their own dedicated bandwidth. Second, the switch knows the address of each user and can store packets of information in its memory until the user is free to receive the message. It eliminates the need for information to be passed through everyone connected to the network thereby allowing remaining nodes on the network to remain free to exchange additional information. This cuts down on time and the possibility of collisions.

Kingston’s 10/100 Switch

Whether used to connect individual power users or segment networks to provide dedicated bandwidth, switches can meet the demands of any size network. Because Kingston EtheRx switches support both 10Mbps and 100 Mbps Ethernet networks, or a combination of both, they are the perfect solution for users looking for a way to cost-effectively migrate to Fast Ethernet.

Kingston’s EtheRx 10/100 switches come with 4 or 8 ports of dedicated auto-sensing connectivity. Since no configuration is required, installation is quick and easy.

All of Kingston’s switches utilize a store-and-forward technology for transferring packets of information. This means that each packet is completely checked for errors before it is delivered to its destination thereby eliminating the transfer of faulty packets. Store-and-forward assures both reliability and efficiency.

Full duplexing is another characteristic of Kingston’s switches, which make them a valuable asset to any network. The typical shared ethernet network utilizes half-duplexing, meaning that a workstation may either transmit or receive data but cannot do both at the same time. Full duplexing allows for transmission and receiving to occur simultaneously thereby allowing throughput of up to 200Mbps on a 100Mbps connection when a computer is connected directly to a switching port.

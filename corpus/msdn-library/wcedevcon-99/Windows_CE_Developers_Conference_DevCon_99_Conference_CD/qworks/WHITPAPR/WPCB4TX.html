Highlights

The Fast EtheRx CardBus PC Card 10/100TX Fast Ethernet Adapter, model KNE-CB4TX, is a 32-bit high-performance credit-card sized adapter that allows any portable computer with a PC Card Type II or Type III slot that supports CardBus operation to attach to an Ethernet or a Fast Ethernet network. The Fast EtheRx CardBus PC Card adapter complies with the Personal Computer Memory Card International Association (PCMCIA) PC Card Standard, February 1995, and the IEEE 802.3i 10BASE-T, 802.3u 100BASE-TX and IEEE 802.3 CSMA/CD standards.

The KNE-CB4TX is designed for 100BASE-TX Fast Ethernet and 10BASE-T Ethernet networks using an RJ-45 connector for Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) Category 5 cabling. For ease of installation, the Fast EtheRx CardBus PC Card auto-negotiates 10/100 Mbps speed selection and half- or full-duplex mode operation. It can be connected to a LAN using the supplied Media Access Module (MAM). Kingston also includes a Media Coupler and 6ft CAT 5 UTP cable to extend the length of the UTP cable connection.

What is CardBus technology?

As mobile computing gains popularity, the effort to make laptops equivalent to their desktop counterparts continues. Traditionally, the customization of the mobile platform has been more difficult to achieve than the desktop because of the need to integrate many system components into a very small area. There arose a need to add-on functionality to the laptop platform much like the ability to add cards to the desktop for networking, video, sound, etc… This need was met through the use of a LAN or modem PC card. PC cards, designed to be about the size and shape of a credit card, act as the interface for expanding memory, storage, and I/O capabilities of portable computer systems.

As desktop technology continued to advance, there grew a need to advance the functionality of the laptop as well. To address these needs, in November of 1998, PCMCIA announced that it has officially approved the PCI power management specification into the PC card standard. This new standard is referred to as CardBus technology and brings desktop PCI power management features to the laptop environment. The CardBus card possesses the same physical characteristics as the standard Type III PC card and was conceived as a means of adding higher bandwidth capabilities to the notebook.

 

CardBus vs. PC Card

The Personal Computer Memory Card International Association (PCMCIA) describes the difference between the PC card and CardBus as follows:

PC Card-16 cards use an 8- or 16-bit interface that operates at ISA bus speeds (8 MHz) using an ISA-like asynchronous protocol. In contrast, CardBus provides a 32-bit multiplexed address/data path, which operates at PCI local-bus speeds of up to 33 MHz, yielding a peak bandwidth of 132MB/sec. CardBus accomplishes this by adopting the synchronous burst-transfer orientation of PCI, as well as a bus protocol, which is essentially identical to that of PCI.

CardBus is a 32-bit interface that provides 100Mbps capabilities for LAN, SCSI II, video conferencing and other high performance needs. By taking advantage of internal bus speeds, the CardBus can operate at speeds six times faster than its 16-bit PC card predecessor and five times faster than a 100Mbps PC card. In addition, CardBus PC cards operate at 3.3 volts thereby saving power and prolonging battery life.

PCI Performance

By utilizing a 32-bit interface, the KNE-CB4TX achieves speeds up to six times faster than the traditional 16-bit PC card and yields a bandwidth of up to 132MB per second.

Bus-mastering capability

Kingston’s KNE-CB4TX features bus mastering capabilities allowing it to assume control of the system’s bus making data transfer quick and efficient. In contrast, the 16-bit PC card can act only as a bus slave and must pass data through the CPU before transferring it to the host system. This slows down data transfer and places more strain on the processor.

Ease of use

The 10/100 CardBus inherits the PC card’s plug-n-play capabilities through its configuration software known as Card and Socket Services. PCMCIA says:

Card and Socket Services provides automatic allocation of system resources that may be needed by an add-in. This PnP capability eliminates a major source of system configuration errors, greatly increasing the system’s "user-friendliness".

Hot Swapping Capability

This software also provides for the CardBus’ hot-swapping capability which allows for the removal of one card and the insertion of another without having to reset or turn off the computer. Once a card is removed, Socket Services instructs Card Services to re-allocate system resources for other needs.

Low Voltage

Perhaps one of the biggest concerns of the mobile PC user is the length of time they are able to run their system without needing to recharge it or utilize a power supply. Unlike the stationary desktop models, the laptop’s functionality is limited to the length of the system’s battery life. Thermal dissipation is a related issue in that laptops have limited means of extinguishing excessive internal heat, which is confined within the system chassis. High temperatures can result in disruption of the system’s operation and may cause permanent damage to the system’s components. By limiting the amount of power consumption, the battery life is prolonged and temperatures are reduced. Kingston’s CardBus addresses these issues by operating at a low 3.3 volts of power.

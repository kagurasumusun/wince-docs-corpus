Kingston Increases Fast Ethernet Margins

Fountain Valley, CA— (March 27, 1997), While other Ethernet adapter manufacturers are decreasing both reseller and end user prices and thereby cutting dealer margins, Kingston Technology today announced that they have slashed suggested reseller price for their popular EtheRx PCI 10/100 Fast Ethernet adapters to $63 and have left MSRP unchanged at $109. The result is an unprecedented 42% dealer margin on the product.

"Kingston networking works exclusively through the dealer channel," explained Jamie Stack, Networking Business Unit, Sales Manager. "Increasing dealer margins while our competitors squeeze the channel is our way of demonstrating this commitment."

The EtheRx PCI 10/100 Fast Ethernet adapter features N-Way Auto-Negotiation which automatically configures the adapter for 10Mbps or 100Mbps operation, depending on the current line speed. The bus-mastering design optimizes throughput while maintaining low CPU utilization. Additionally, QStartä , Kingston’s unique GUI, configuration and diagnostic program, makes Network Operating System configuration virtually automatic. The EtheRx PCI 10/100 adapter allows for maximum flexibility: 10Mbps operation today and 100Mbps upgradability for the future.

Based on the industry-leading Digital Semiconductor 10/100Mbps PCI Fast Ethernet controller, the EtheRx PCI 10/100 offers unmatched hardware compatibility. To make the migration to Fast Ethernet seamless, Kingston ships the EtheRx PCI 10/100 with a complete suite of drivers: ODI for DOS, Novell Server Drivers, NDIS2 for DOS, NDIS2 for OS/2, NDIS3, Packet Drivers and SCO LLI drivers. Certification is pending for all popular Network Operating Systems including Novell Netware and Microsoft Windows 95 and Windows NT.

With sales of more than $1 billion for 1996, Kingston Technology Company is the recognized industry leader in memory products for PCs, workstations, servers, notebooks and laser printers. The recent formation of the Kingston Networking Unit marks the manufacturer’s successful entry into the Ethernet market.

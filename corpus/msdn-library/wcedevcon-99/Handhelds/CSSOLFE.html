Connectivity and Remote Access

Mobile professionals and entrepreneurs must be in constant touch with their mission-critical information. This means that they need easy connections to e-mail, faxes, and the Internet. They also need to create, edit, review, present and print documents while on the road. Pocket Carbon Copy, Pocket Internet Explorer, and other C-Series programs provide remote internet access. Windows CE offers Ethernet access with an optional Ethernet PC-Card. The Compaq developed Ethernet utility allows complete browsing through the network zones very similar to Windows Explorer. Making an Ethernet Cross-Over Cable for Networking Two Computers without a Hub

Overview: Making a Hubless Two-node Network courtesy of Socket Communications (www.socketcom.com)

The following paragraphs describe how to wire an RJ-45 10BaseT cable so that two Ethernet adapters can communicate directly, without an intermediate hub, in a two-node network. This can be useful if, for example, you are at a hotel or a trade show and you want to use your Low Power Ethernet Card to synchronize your C-Series with a partner notebook, without the hassle of using a hub.


Why You Need a Cross-Over Cable

A 10BaseT connection requires two pairs of unshielded twisted pair wires, a total of four wires. One pair of wires is called the transmit pair (TD+ and TD-) and the other pair is called the receive pair (RD+ and RD-). Because adapters and hubs use opposite pairs to transmit and receive, standard 10BaseT cables use straight-through wiring. This allows PCs connected to 10BaseT hubs to communicate without conflict.

When you connect two notebooks together, or a handheld PC to a notebook, or a hub to a hub, you must use a cross-over cable. This connects the pins used as the transmit pair at one end of the cable to the pins used as the receive pair at the other end. The easiest way to make a cross-over cable is to start with a standard cable and replace the RJ-45 connector at one end. The pinouts for a cross-over cable are shown below.

Note: Although the functional equivalent of a cross-over cable is required to connect two hubs to one another, many hubs have a cross-over switch that allows you to use a straight-through cable for this purpose.


Pin Assignments for a Cross-Over Cable

Pin 1 of the RJ-45 connector is on the left as you look straight at the connector with the pins down and the cable behind. This is shown in the picture below:

Swap the pins on one connector according to the diagram below:
Pin 1 TD + <—> Pin 3 RD +
Pin 2 TD - <—> Pin 6 RD -
Pin 3 RD + <—> Pin 1 TD +
Pin 6 RD - <—> Pin 2 TD -

It is a good idea to put red tape around your new cross-over cable so people don't confuse it with a straight-through cable.

Remember: You cannot use a cross-over cable to network your PC to a hub, and you cannot use a standard straight-through cable to network your PC directly to another PC.

MAIL ON THE RUN!

It has been determined that corporate users of handheld PCs demand access to their legacy mail systems. For this reason, Compaq has elected to bundle a single seat Client and Agent mail access application. The Mail on the Run! software developed by River Run Software Group® offers unprecedented remote access to LAN-based e-mail systems. The Mail on the Run! for Windows CE product enables C-Series to connect — directly, or over telephone, circuit-switched cellular or wireless networks — with the world's most popular mail systems, including Microsoft® Mail, Microsoft Exchange Server, Lotus® Notes™ Mail and Lotus cc:Mail™, and Novell GroupWise®.

The Mail on the Run! for Windows CE software provides an automatic connection to your LAN-based e-mail system. Seamlessly integrated into the Windows CE Inbox application, Mail on the Run! software can send, receive, store, forward, and reply to messages on your e-mail system. Users may process their e-mail while on-line, download copies of all the mail, or download headers and mark them to be copied, moved or deleted the next time they connect. In addition, the Mail on the Run! product also provides sophisticated support for file attachments, automatically converting files into native file formats, and giving users the ability to set limits on how much they wish to download. The Mail on the Run! application is designed for the most efficient e-mail processing possible in order to minimize user actions and connect time. When you load the Mail on the Run! software on your C-Series, you will find that it becomes completely integrated into the Windows CE Inbox and provides easy access to the Contacts application. Once you know how to use these Windows CE functions, you immediately know how to use Mail on the Run!.

To get started, double tap the Inbox icon from the main Windows CE screen. This takes you to the Inbox application. Then select a mail service by tapping Options under the Compose menu and tap the Service tab which displays the mail services installed on your H/PC. This is where you select the LAN-based e-mail system you will connect to, and by following the prompts provided, set up your communications and mail properties, and customize your Inbox and mail options.

If no services are installed, the Installed services box will be empty. To add a mail service to the Inbox application, tap the Add button to display the Services dialog box.

Select a service from the Choose one box and click the OK button and follow directions.

Once you have set all the properties, it is simple to compose a message from the Inbox. Tap the New Message icon or select New Message from the Compose menu to display the Send Note dialog box.

To address the message, tap the Contacts icon to display the Choose Address dialog box.

All recipients that you have entered into the Contacts applications are listed with their e-mail addresses. You may select entries for the To, Cc and Bcc fields. Then you can compose your message. When you are finished, tap Send under the File menu. If you are connected to your e-mail system, the message will be sent immediately. If you are off-line, the message goes into the Outbox and will be sent the next time that you connect.

To receive mail from your LAN-based e-mail system, simply select your e-mail system under the Service menu of the Inbox screen, and then tap Connect. Each time you tap Connect, Mail on the Run! is connected to the Mail Agent, and sends and retrieves messages based on your preferences. Retrieved messages are displayed in the Inbox as shown below.

The Mail on the Run! for Windows CE product consists of two components: client software that is installed on the C-Series, and Agent software. The Agent processes requests of behalf of all the clients, and communicates with client devices over wireless as well as wireline networks. The Mail on the Run! Agent runs on either a Windows 95 desktop computer with Remote Access Server (RAS) or on a Windows NT system. The Agent can support access by multiple concurrent users and needs to have access to the mail server. RAS provides the security function for users seeking remote access to the e-mail system post office. The illustration below shows the Mail on the Run! Agent main screen.

C-Series users are granted remote access through the Agent; user names are specified through the Users menu. The Agent keeps a log of all e-mail activity that it manages, which can be displayed on the screen (as shown above) and/or stored in a file.

When you connect to the Agent from your H/PC, the Agent confirms your name and password before proceeding. Once your access is approved, the Agent processes your requests; when finished, your H/PC will automatically log off and disconnect.

The wireless networks supported for communication between the Windows CE client and the Agent include ARDIS™, CDPD™, GSM, and DataTAC 5000™.

POP3/SMTP vs Mail on the Run!

Recently, popular LAN-based e-mail systems have introduced support for the Internet mail protocol, POP3. Lotus was the first to announce POP3 support with Notes 4.5/Domino. Now, both Microsoft Exchange Server (version 5.0) and Lotus cc:Mail (version 8.0) tout this capability.

The following paragraphs compare Mail on the Run! with POP3 access to corporate mail systems, and demonstrates Mail on the Run! added functionality for use by mobile professionals.
¹ A byte-by-byte comparison of POP3 access to cc:Mail and Lotus Notes 4.5/Domino vs Mail on the Run! version 2.3 is provided in a section titled Transmission Statistics.

Superior Remote Access

Mail on the Run! uses a protocol that is highly optimized to communicate reliably and cost-effectively over wired and wireless networks. Only the amount of data requested by the user is downloaded to the C-Series. Mail on the Run! options such as the ability to filter out unwanted messages, the ability to truncate messages beyond a certain size, and the ability to prevent or limit attachments directly translates into lower connection times and savings in time and money for the user.

POP3, on the other hand, is a mail protocol that works well across high-speed networks but is not optimized for low-bandwidth WANs. Even when the user previews headers of messages, large amounts of data have to be and are downloaded to the handheld PC¹.


File Attachments

Mail on the Run! provides sophisticated support for file attachments. Files are automatically converted into native file formats such as Pocket Word and Pocket Excel when they are downloaded to the C-Series. Options are available to set a limit on the size of attachments to be downloaded, or prevent downloading of attachments altogether. Since the handheld PC file formats have a smaller foot-print than their desktop counterparts, file attachments are significantly reduced in size before being transmitted to the C-Series (Word-to-Pocket Word. Conversions result in reductions to a third or even one-tenth of the original size, depending on the content).

The POP3 client on Windows CE does not support file attachments in messages. Encode/decode solutions require manual steps to be carried out to send and receive attachments. Encoding procedures (such as UUENCODE, or BASE64) convert the contents of a binary file into printable characters before transmitting over the Internet (which inherently supports only ASCII printable characters). This significantly increases the size of file attachments, resulting in longer connection times and increased cost to the user.


Address Book Support

Mail on the Run! provides remote access to the mail system's address books on the LAN. A Mail on the Run! user can preview and/or download entries from the address book to the Contacts application on the C-Series from a remote location. The user can also set a rule to automatically add the name and e-mail address of the sender from messages downloaded onto the C-Series.

These features are not available with POP3 solutions.

Cost-Effective over Wireless

Mail on the Run!'s efficient protocol translates into savings for the user, as most wireless packet-data networks such as ARDIS, CDPD, and RAM Mobile charge the customer on the amount of data transmitted over the network. The user can control the amount of data transmitted by applying filtering options and truncation limits on messages that are downloaded.

POP3 solutions for Windows CE currently only support a dial-up network.


Reliable over Wireless

Mail on the Run!, being 'network-aware', has built-in reliability which takes into consideration network-specific idiosyncrasies. Its intelligent retry logic for ARDIS that prevents network congestion is just one example of the reliability features built into Mail on the Run!

Mail on the Run! also provides a visual indication of progress, signal strength, and other modem information for all supported wireless modems.

POP3 solutions for Windows CE currently only support dial-up networks.

Encryption

Mail on the Run! provides the user an option to transparently encrypt messages when they are transmitted over the network. This feature is exclusive to Mail on the Run! and is not supported by the current POP3 clients, where messages are transmitted in 'clear-text' format; anyone who intercepts the message can read it. Mail on the Run! uses a variant of industry-standard DES encryption optimized for the handheld PC to ensure secure communication.


Mail Alert!

Mail Alert!, provided as an option with Mail on the Run!, allows the user to be paged when certain messages are received in his/her mailbox. Mail Alert!'s powerful 'push' capability automatically redirects the first few lines of critical e-mail to users equipped with alpha-numeric pagers. The user can change the rules that trigger Mail Alert! on-the-fly from a remote location.

Mail on the Run!/Mail Alert! works with all popular paging service providers. Mail Alert! will soon be available for GSM users through Short Messaging Service (SMS), provided by GSM operators: users will be automatically notified when a message is sent, and the first few lines of their e-mail will be displayed on the LCD screen of their cell-phones.


Scheduling, Calendaring, and more!

River Run's In Sync!™ technology integrated with Mail on the Run! provides users the ability to remotely access group scheduling and calendaring (S&C) data in addition to mail. In Sync!™ allows users to keep the Calendar, Contacts, and Tasks applications on their H/PCs in sync with S&C data stored on Lotus Notes, Lotus Organizer or Microsoft Schedule+.

Mail on the Run! also provides remote access to other popular mail systems such as Novell GroupWise.

This section contains a byte-by-byte comparison of Mail on the Run! with POP3 access on Windows CE. The number of bytes transmitted on the network is directly proportional to the response time and cost to the user.

TEST SETUP

Mail on the Run! data was collected using a Compaq handheld PC running Mail on the Run! version 2.3. The Mail on the Run! Agent software was run on a Windows NT 4.0 Workstation.

POP3 data was collected using the same Compaq handheld PC with its built-in POP3 client.

Data was collected for mail transactions with Lotus Notes 4.5/Domino and Lotus cc:mail 6.0. Access to cc:Mail 6.0 through POP3 was made using MicroBurst's Virtual Courier V1.0.

The transmission data was collected by checking the byte count available on the Windows NT RAS Server (Programs > Administrative Tools > Remote Access Admin > Communication Ports > Port Status > Byte Count).

In all cases, real messages were used without attachments and truncation limits were set to a very high value to prevent truncation of the messages being used for testing.

Synchronization

The C-Series allows mobile professionals to synchronize C-Series data with the data on their desktop PCs. This is far better than simple upload/download capabilities. Only the latest most up-to-date information on each computer is retained without requiring the user to select which information is most recent — in the case of conflicts the user gets a choice to select which information is the most up-to-date. E-mail, word processing, calendar, Schedule +, Lotus Notes, Lotus Organizer, and other files are updated on the base PC and on the C-Series when the two units are connected. The ActiveSync™ program provides full compatibility with Microsoft Windows and the Microsoft Office suite including Outlook contacts, email and attachment, tasks, and other PIM files. In addition, ActiveSync technology automatically synchronizes the data at pre-determined intervals when the C-Series is continuously connected to the base desktop PC. Users can synchronize their data with two desktop PCs (or one desktop and one server), if necessary. This means that they can keep data up to date at the office and at home. One desktop computer can synchronize with up to 1,000 C-Series handheld PCs. Using this feature, an entire sales team could share a single desktop PC.

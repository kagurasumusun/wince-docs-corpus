INTEGRATING THE C-SERIES
INTO THE CORPORATE IS NETWORK
Pre-Implementation Planning

Each corporate user group has unique needs. Laptop and Notebook computers meet the needs of users requiring full-function PCs with full-screen displays. The handheld PC meets the needs of another group: mobile professionals requiring a small sized, lightweight, low-cost unit that provides the needed functionality for remote access, email, and other on-the-road tasks. As with Laptops and Notebooks, handheld PCs can be successfully integrated into the corporate IS network.

The Compaq C-Series handheld PC is designed to excel as a highly mobile remote access and e-mail solution for mobile executives. It also solves many other specific problems for this group. For example, users can originate new files or make changes to existing files while on the road, and easily synchronize C-Series files (including calendar files) with the files back in their offices. These rare accomplishments make a profound difference in the way mobile professionals work. That is why the C-Series is the first professional handheld PC. Besides being the best solution, LAN administrators or corporate IS managers will be pleased to know that The C-Series uses a very stable operating system. Integrating Windows CE into the network infrastructure is a straightforward process that makes it easy to get started right. A highly experienced and knowledgeable support staff makes Compaq the best partner to help with this process. The following sections are designed to help prepare a written corporate implementation plan.


Participation Planning

While the C-Series has many virtues, it is best to focus on a couple of areas that stand to gain the most, like messaging and remote access. Where do you start? Determine the department or division that will benefit the most, and plan to start implementation there. In the health care industry, the doctors that walk the hospital halls would be a good starting point. The insurance industry might want to start with the sales agents in the filed, or with the mobile claims adjusters. There are many other comparable examples in other industries.


Installation Supervision

Once the pilot group is determined, it is just a matter of ensuring that the C-Series units are issued to all of the appropriate personnel, and installing any special software. Some simple training classes will encourage most efficient use of the C-Series units, and will help minimize service and technical support calls.


Conversion Planning

It is best to provide a written implementation plan for all new C-Series users. Expect the new users to include people from three groups: (1) those who have used other handheld computers or personal information managers, (2) those who now use paper-based systems, and (3) those who have not used an organized method of keeping track of information before. Be sure that those who have used other handheld systems understand the superiority of the C-Series units, and the importance of immediately transferring all information from the old system. For those new users accustomed to using a paper-based system for keeping track of appointments and contacts, it is important to stress that the transition from paper to digital form is very easy. It is best to make the conversion from the manual system immediately so that the benefits of the new system can be gained immediately by all. Emphasize the benefits of having everything organized and accessible through use of the C-Series unit to new users who have not used an organized method of keeping track of tasks and contacts before. Following written implementation plans and organizing inbox, calendar, contacts, tasks, documents, and spreadsheets will make jobs easier.


Getting the System Operating

Virtually no effort is required to get basic applications like Microsoft Word and Excel up and running. They are already installed, so it is just a matter of putting them to use. Special versions of Visual Basic and Visual C++ can be used to develop specialized C-Series applications for your company. For help in developing custom applications, see the handheld PC Home Page at www.microsoft.com/windowsce/hpc. More information can be found in a companion White Paper highlighting the technical aspects of the C-Series. An independent software vendor setup utility allows you to easily set up and administer these and other software applications. Troubleshooting is easy, and help is always available through Compaq's technical support staff.


Assigning Responsibilities

In order for implementation of the C-Series to be successful at your company, specific personnel will need to be assigned the following responsibilities:

  • Written Implementation Plan;
  • Integration of C-Series into corporate IS network;
  • Development of custom applications, if needed;
  • Installation of custom software, if any;
  • Training of new users;
  • Assignment of initial and secondary user groups; and
  • Technical support for C-Series users.
Installing All System Features

It is important that new users start with programs that will help them the most. In most cases, this means that they should be trained to use the C-Series remote access and e-mail features first. Training should continue on a regular schedule, after the initial applications are mastered. A typical corporate quarterly training schedule would include classes for new and advanced users. New users would focus on basic programs like Inbox, Pocket Outlook, and Mail on the Run. Advanced classes might vary each quarter, alternating instruction on use of the Calendar, Contacts, and Tasks programs with classes teaching Microsoft Pocket Word, Pocket Excel, and Pocket PowerPoint. Most of these programs are similar to the ones used on desktop computers, so training programs are already in place at many companies.

Since their beginnings as personal information managers (PIMs), both palm-size and handheld PCs have become an essential part of the technological arsenal of fast-paced, mobile business professionals. But recent improvements to Microsoft Windows CE have made it possible to create a new class of handheld devices that combine the mobility and ease of use of the palm-size PC with the larger displays and keyboards found on notebook computers.

These new Windows CE devices are easily connected to corporate networks, intranets, and extranets using wired or wireless access. The new connectivity options, in turn, have opened a whole new world of enterprise applications to mobile workers — from the traditional sales reps and technicians working in the field, to a whole new class of data gatherers who can sync automatically to complex back-end databases and applications sitting behind the firewall.

To answer the growing demand for this type of sophisticated functionality, Compaq created the Compaq Aero 8000® Handheld PC Professional — an advanced unit that supports the most cutting-edge enterprise integration technologies available on the market today. This white paper explores the evolution of the Handheld PC Pro and explains in detail the technologies and applications that are helping Compaq blaze a trail through this increasingly important new market area.


EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The field of mobile computing has received a major impetus from recent improvements in the Windows CE operating system. In particular, the class of mobile devices using Windows CE Handheld PC Professional Edition 2.11 is finding increased usage in a variety of enterprise applications, including sales-force automation, data collection, and thin client connectivity.
Most devices in this class have the look and feel of notebook computers, but with many important differences — including extremely light weight, instant on/off capability, lower cost, and longer battery life. Advanced devices in this class, such as the Compaq Aero 8000® Handheld PC Professional, include even more important options — such as Smart Card readers, built-in high-speed modems, and networking capabilities — that make them ideal for deployment in sophisticated enterprise solutions.
Rather than replacing notebook devices, these mobile computers are finding a niche as the platform of choice in situations where notebooks are too expensive or high-powered, yet palm-size computers are not sufficiently powerful or easy-to-use. Interestingly, these new devices are presenting unique opportunities for delivering just the right amount of power for mobile and task-specific applications, including:
  • Enterprise messaging and collaboration
  • Mobile thin client solutions
  • Mainframe data access and terminal emulation
  • Enterprise client-server database connectivity
  • Remote access (RAS), wireless, and Internet connectivity
The growing list of high-powered solutions available for handheld integration points to an increasingly bright future for this class of computing devices. Until now, mobile computing devices were used primarily for their personal information manager (PIM) applications, which include such standard functionality as contact management, to-do lists, and appointment scheduling. But as demand grows for mobile computers, users will need advanced equipment that can handle the changing dynamics of an increasingly demanding business environment. Today's mobile professional must juggle not only meeting schedules and contacts, but a heavy load of e-mail, competitive data, financial reports, and other information delivered through global networks.

In designing the Aero 8000®, Compaq created a product that answers these needs and takes the handheld PC to the next level. The resulting product combines all the power of enterprise computing in a single convenient platform.

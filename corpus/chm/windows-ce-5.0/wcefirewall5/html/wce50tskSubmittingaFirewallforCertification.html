Submitting a Firewall for Certification

Certification is a way of validating that your firewall is solid according to industry standards, and that it works properly to protect a home computer or intranet. Firewalls differ based on their design goals and what they need to protect, and can range from offering basic IP protection to application-level protection. Certifying your firewall verifies that the device passes specific functionality tests that are recognized by the firewall industry. Certification also proves that your firewall functions correctly for its intended use.

Firewall certification is done by a certification agency. Typically, the agency performs custom certification tests that take into account the firewall design principles. Each agency has its own criteria for the functionality that your firewall design must offer, and for the items you must include in the certification packet that you submit.

The following list shows the items that a submission packet typically includes:

  • Your firewall device.
  • A checklist with the results of a pre-test that you have run on the device.
  • The written testing agreement, sometimes called a Security Policy. This policy helps define the firewall rules, as well as deal with legal and non-compliance issues.
  • Documentation that supports the firewall.

Best Practices

Know how the firewall works, and why it works the way it does, as follows:

  • Determine the protective relationships that the firewall implements. Consider how the firewall protects itself from attack, and how it protects attached networks from attack.
  • Consider the strengths and weaknesses in and behind the firewall.
  • Consider management strategy. For example, remote access to a firewall by use of a password could interject a weakness into the firewall that is not evident through testing.
  • Consider the verification process during the planning phase of your firewall device to reduce required changes during the certification process.

Comprehensively test your firewall for vulnerabilities, as follows:

  • Determine the basic assumptions of the firewall, and then derive tests to validate these assumptions.
  • Based on the high-level design, look for weaknesses in the firewall implementation. Attempt to penetrate areas that are critical to the firewall functioning, including components with which the firewall interacts. Attempt to penetrate the firewall using common Internet attacks.
  • Ensure the firewall functions properly by verifying the following:
    • The firewall does the things that it should do.
    • It does not do things that is should not do.
    • It handles failures appropriately. Test default settings to ensure functionality.

Consider certification during your firewall product cycle, as follows:

  • Consider the products that a certification agency verifies before choosing that agency to certify your firewall.
  • Ask the certification agency for sample reports. These reports show tests that the agency uses.
  • Leave time to make fixes that the certification process may require.

To submit a firewall for certification

  1. Decide on a firewall certification agency.
  2. Based on the requirements of the certifying agency, create the documentation to include in the submission packet.

    Typically, this includes a checklist of claims to be tested and a testing agreement or Security Policy.

  3. Send the submission packet, including the firewall device and other requirements, to the certification agency for testing.
